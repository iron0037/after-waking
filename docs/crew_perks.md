# Crew Perk Trees (AW-95) **[DECIDED: first pass, 2026-10-23]**

Numbers and names will be tuned in play; the structure and principles are settled.

This builds on crew slice 1 (AW-94): officers already gain levels from doing their jobs, and each level gives a small passive bonus at that station. Perk trees are the second layer.

## Principles

1. **Levels make you better at your job; perks change what you can do.** Level bonuses are the steady numbers, such as drill rate or fold fuel. Perks avoid adding more of those except in one deliberate form (rule 2). Most perks add a new option, remove a restriction, or change a rule: "prospect from two hexes away", "land anywhere in a region", "the first incident each expedition costs no time".
2. **Cross-training is how overlap becomes a feature.** Some perks let one officer support another station. They count as **extra effective levels at that other station, for one named bonus only**. For example, the physicist's *Drive Harmonics* counts as +2 navigation levels for fold fuel. This reuses the level table, so no new numbers need balancing, and it stays readable: "fold fuel −24% (navigator level 9 + 2 from your physicist)". Effective levels can exceed 10, up to 12.
3. **Choices that matter:**
   - One perk point per level gained (levels 2–10 give 9 points).
   - Each station has 3 branches of 5 tiers. Tier *n* needs *n*−1 perks already taken in that branch.
   - Nine points buys one complete branch plus most of a second, never all three, so two navigators can end up very different.
4. **Hiring for specialty.** Recruits come with their points already spent, usually in their clan's line: a Digger engineer arrives with Mechanics perks, a Helm navigator with Interstellar ones. The hiring hall becomes "who do I need", not only "who is highest level".
5. **Retraining.** The Lyceum runs retraining courses aboard the Intrepid. Weeks of time and some credits refund an officer's points. This is the "crew training course: weeks to months" item from the time budget.
6. **Built when their system exists.** Perks that depend on combat (M3), outposts and clans (M4), aliens (M6), probes (AW-16), boats (AW-17) or refining (AW-18) appear in the tree as **locked: "arrives with …"**. Tiers that work today ship first.

Legend: ✅ works with today's systems · ⏳ needs a later system (named)

## Leveling up is an event

- **A celebration.** When an officer (or the captain) gains a level, a small popup marks it: who, the new level, what the level gives, and the raise in their wage.
- **A perk prompt.** The popup offers a perk choice right away. The player can pick now or **bank the point** for later.
- **Banked points show.** Unspent points appear as a badge on the top bar's Crew button and on that person's roster card until they're spent.

## Crew sentiment: happiness and loyalty **[DECIDED 2026-10-23]**

Each officer has two opinions of the captain:

| | Happiness | Loyalty |
|---|---|---|
| Timescale | Short term: recent events | Long term: slow to build or lose |
| Goes up with | Finds, level-ups, bonuses, shore leave, a profitable trip | Sustained happiness, bonuses, and actions that favor their clan (contracts for it, buying its gear, siding with it in clan events) |
| Goes down with | Long stretches away from the Intrepid, dull or losing trips, wages below the going rate for their level, incidents and injuries | Sustained unhappiness, working against their clan |
| Starting value | Middling | From clan: your own clan starts high (once the captain has a starting clan; see Starting Clan Perks in game_design.md), Freebooters start low, others in between |

- **Coupling.** Happiness changes nudge loyalty, slowly.
- **Quitting.** The lower happiness plus loyalty, the likelier someone is to give notice at the next docking, or to desert in a crisis. Young crew are more restless.
- **Visibility.** By default the roster shows each as low / medium / high. The captain's **Read the Room** perk shows exact 0–100 scores.

### v1 as built (2026-10-24, `src/sim/sentiment.ts`)

**Happiness**
- **Drift:** relaxes toward 55, about 3% of the gap per day.
- **Shore leave:** at the Intrepid it adds 1.5 per day, up to 80.
- **Long hauls:** after 45 days away it falls 0.4 per day.
- **Events:**

  | Event | Effect |
  |---|---|
  | Level-up | +8 to that officer |
  | Finds | +1 each to science, up to +2 per sweep |
  | Sample collected | +1 to medical |
  | Incident | −1 to all, plus −2 to engineering and −1 to medical |
  | Rescue | −8 to all |
  | Trip profit or loss, at docking | ±6 to all |
  | Unpaid wages at docking | −8 to all (and −2 loyalty) |
  | Contract done or lapsed | ±2 to all |

**Loyalty**
- **Drift:** follows happiness at ±0.05 per day (at happiness 100 or 0).
- **Clan:** contracts done or lapsed for a clan give its officers ±4; buying a clan's gear gives its officers +2.
- **Bonuses:** up to +2 per payout.
- **Starting values:** 50 for the starting crew, 40 for new hires, and 25 or 20 for Freebooters.

**Notice and leaving** (combined score = average of happiness and loyalty)
- **Notice:** given at docking when the score drops below 30 (35 for crew under 30 years old).
- **Leaving:** at the next docking they leave if it's still below 35, or withdraw their notice at 40 or more. The post stays empty until someone is signed.
- **Miserable** (happiness below 25): they work one level below their real level.

**Perks:** Morale (shore leave twice as fast, good news +50%), Loyalty (loyalty gains +50%) and Read the Room (exact scores).

**Career report:**
- **Competent:** pays a 5% share; crew happiness stays around 80 and loyalty reaches its maximum in about 3 years; nobody quits.
- **Novice:** pays nothing; happiness is around 70 and loyalty 45–60, with about 1 quit per career late on.

### Bonuses: a share of the spoils

- **A setting.** The captain chooses what share of each trip's profit goes to the crew as a bonus (0–20%).
- **Effect.** Bonuses raise happiness and, over time, loyalty. Zero isn't a penalty, since they're salaried, but bonuses help when you can afford them.
- **Split.** Paid at the Intrepid when a trip's sales settle, divided among officers by level and among the enlisted crew.

## Enlisted crew **[PROPOSED, to design]**

- **A head count.** Beyond the five officers, the ship carries a number of enlisted crew, spread across all the jobs aboard. They're not individuals: one count, one wage, and their own collective happiness and loyalty.
- **Each hull sets a minimum and a maximum** (see ship classes in game_design.md).
- **Trade-offs to settle:**
  - **Bigger crew:** more drilling shifts per day, faster loading, damage-control and boarding parties in combat (M3), and the ability to run a second rover or a refinery.
  - **Smaller crew:** lower wages, fewer consumables, less mass, and a crowd that's easier to keep happy.
  - **Below the minimum:** stations slow down.
- **Hiring.** Hands come from the hiring hall in bulk, with no names.
